## What we know so far (from search snippets only -- not verified against
full text, do not treat as ground truth)

- Standard reversal measures appear to have weakened in international
  equity markets because they mix firm-specific and industry-wide return
  components.
- When measured *relative to industry peers* (industry-adjusted), the
  reversal effect persists.
- Sample: 64 countries.
- Conventional (non-adjusted) strategy: negligible returns.
- Industry-adjusted strategy: 0.53%/month, Sharpe ratio 0.74.
- Keywords: short-term reversal, industry-adjusted reversal, intra-industry
  reversal, regret anomaly, international equity markets, return
  predictability, cross-section of stock returns.

This is promising as a companion/contrast to paper #2 (which finds
standard, non-adjusted reversal works fine on large-cap US/European
stocks) -- but the actual methodology (how "industry" is defined, what
universe, formation/holding periods, how the 64 countries' data was
sourced) is unknown until the full text is read.
